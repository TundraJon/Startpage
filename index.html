1. Welcome

Hi! This page is yours. Every button, every category, every color can be changed, moved, or removed. This guide walks through everything in the order you'd naturally use it. You can always come back and read it again by tapping the ? at the top of the page.

2. What's Already Here

The first time you ever open this page, it comes with a bunch of categories and buttons already filled in — kind of like a backpack that already has a few things packed in it. You can keep any of it, change it, or clear it all out and start from scratch. It's completely yours.

3. The Top of the Page

The very top of the page — the title, search bar, clock, and weather card — stays in place while everything else scrolls underneath it, so it's always there when you need it.

  • The search bar searches the whole internet. You can tap to switch between Google and Bing.
  • The sun/moon button switches between light mode and dark mode. For Auto — switches automatically, light in the morning (7am) and dark in the evening (7pm), based on your own clock — turn it on in Settings, under Theme (section 13). Tapping the sun/moon button yourself always turns Auto off and takes over manually.
  • The ? button opens this guide.
  • Your profile picture, in the top right, opens Settings (more on that in section 13).

4. The Home Category

Home is the first category on the page, and it's a little different from the others:

  • It's always open — you can't close it or delete it.
  • It has your Clock and Weather card built in, side by side, plus a row of everyday buttons underneath (like Gmail and Maps).
  • Home has its own small row of three buttons at the top: a magnifying glass 🔎 to search your tiles (section 10), a + that opens a small menu for adding a new tile, category, or grouping label (more in sections 6, 7, and 9), and an up-arrow ▲ that instantly closes every open category on the page and scrolls you back to the top.
  • Home can have its own special background color and text color, separate from every other category (see section 14).
  • Long-pressing Home's title bar opens Home Settings — sort Home's own buttons into a different order, manage Home's grouping labels, or pick Home's own background/text color, all in one place.

5. Opening Categories

Tap a category to open it. Here's the one important thing to know: only one category (or one chain of category → subcategory) is ever open at a time. Opening a new one automatically closes whatever was open before. If a category has subcategories inside it, tapping it shows its own buttons first; tapping one of its subcategories swaps you into that one instead. The little button on each category's header can also just tuck away that category's own buttons without fully closing it, if it has subcategories showing.

6. Tiles

Each button on the page is called a tile. When you add one, its little picture (called a favicon) is found automatically from the website — you don't have to do anything. If one can't be found, the tile just shows the name you typed instead, as text.

  • Open whichever category you want the new tile in, then tap Home's + button and choose + Tile. This opens a box where you type the site's name, its web address, and — if you want — a short note (a blurb) and a checkbox for 🇧🇷 Brazil, if it's something Brazilian. (If nothing's open, it adds to Home instead.)
  • A tile with a note shows a small ℹ️ in the corner — tap it to read the note instead of opening the site.
  • A tile marked Brazilian shows a small flag badge in the corner.
  • You can always come back and edit any of this later — the name, the web address, the note, all of it.

7. Grouping Labels

Sometimes you just want to split up the buttons inside one category — like separating "Free" stuff from "Paid" stuff — without making a whole new category. You can add a simple label with a line under it for this. It's just for looks; nothing is attached to it. Add one from Home's + menu (+ Grouping) or from inside that category's edit screen (see section 9). Once you have one, you can also just long-press it right in the grid to jump straight to editing it — rename, delete, or reorder it from there.

8. Selecting, Moving, and Editing Tiles

Long-press any tile to select it — you'll see it get a checkmark and a bar of buttons appears at the bottom: Select All, Cut, Rename/Edit, Paste, Delete, and Clear.

  • Tap other tiles to select more than one at once. Long-press a second tile to select everything in between the first and second.
  • To reorder a tile within the same category: press and hold, let go, then drag it — a separate motion from the initial long-press.
  • To move a tile to a different category: select it, tap Cut, then go open the category you want it in and tap Paste.
  • Rename/Edit opens the same box from section 6, filled in with its current info, ready to change.
  • Delete double-checks with you by name before removing anything.
  • If nothing is selected, this mode closes itself after a few seconds of no activity. Once you've selected something, it waits as long as you need.

Categories work a little differently: long-pressing a category's own title bar takes you straight into the Reorganize tool (next section) with that category already picked.

9. Reorganizing Categories

For bigger changes — like moving a whole category somewhere else, or changing what's nested under what — long-press any category's title bar. This opens a full screen showing every category on the page as a simple, indented list, with the category you long-pressed already picked out for you. From here you can:

  • Tap any row to pick it (tap it again to un-pick it) — this is how you tell the toolbar buttons below which category to work on.
  • Drag the little ☰ handle on a row to reorder it, or drag it sideways to nest it under a different category.
  • Tap the ▼/▶ arrow on a row to show or hide what's nested under it.
  • With a category picked, use the toolbar: 🔧 Properties (edit its name, color, sort order, and groupings), ➕ Category (always adds a new top-level category) or ➕ Subcategory (adds one nested under whichever row is picked), 🗑️ Remove Category, or ▲/▼ to move it up or down among its siblings.
  • Nothing you do here actually changes your page until you tap Save. Tap Cancel anytime to leave with nothing changed.

Home always sits at the very top of this list, greyed out — it can't be picked, moved, renamed, or nested under anything, since everything else is really nested under it anyway.

10. Searching Your Own Tiles

Tap the 🔎 button (next to Home's + button) to search just the buttons you've already added — this never searches the internet, only your own page. Results show up as you type, and each one shows exactly where it lives (like "Games > RPG _ Battle Maps ~ Roll20"). Tap a result and it'll take you right there, with a little glow so it's easy to spot.

11. The Clock

Long-press the clock to open its own settings, with a live preview as you choose:

  • Digital or analog
  • If digital: several color choices (including green, orange, and black-and-white), plus 12-hour or 24-hour
  • If analog: a few different face styles, including a plain 12-hour face, a plain 24-hour face, one with colored number badges, one with two rings (black and red) for 24-hour, and one that shows the real current moon phase right on the face
  • Next to the time, you'll also see your timezone, figured out automatically from your device

12. The Weather Card

Long-press the weather card for its own settings — temperature units, wind units, and which extra details you want to see.

Before weather will work at all, you'll need to add a free WeatherAPI.com key in Settings (see section 13) — this is a one-time setup step.

Once that's set up, the card shows real, live weather: temperature, high/low, feels-like, wind, cloud cover, humidity, dew point, UV index, visibility, moon phase, and the current condition — updating automatically every 15 minutes. It uses your device's real location (or defaults to Orlando, FL if location access isn't available).

  • If there's ever a real severe weather alert for your area, it scrolls across the card. Tap it to dismiss — if it's still active, it'll come back after 2 hours as a reminder.
  • Tap the weather icon to see the next 12 hours, including a temperature graph and chance of rain.
  • The whole card's background can change to match the real weather outside — a day/night sky that shifts with the real sunrise and sunset, twinkling stars at night, drifting clouds sized to match real cloud cover, and animated rain, snow, fog, hail, thunderstorms, and more.

13. Settings

Tap your profile picture (top right) to open Settings. Here's what's there:

  • Site Name — change the page's title
  • Theme — the same light/dark/Auto choice from section 3
  • Profile Photo — pick a photo from your device for your profile picture (or remove it to go back to the default)
  • Backup — see section 15
  • WeatherAPI Key — paste in your free key here (see section 12) — it's kept only on this device, never shared anywhere else

14. Colors

Each top-level category can have its own header color — pick from a few ready-made colors, or choose any color you like. Any subcategories inside it automatically get a lighter version of that same color, so everything stays visually connected without you having to color each one by hand. Home is the one exception — it gets its own separate background color AND its own separate text color, chosen independently from everything else.

15. Saving a Backup

Everything on this page only lives on this one device — there's no account, and nothing is saved anywhere else. If you get a new phone, or your browser's saved data gets cleared, everything would be gone unless you've backed it up first. In Settings, use Backup → Export to save a file with absolutely everything — your categories, tiles, colors, weather key, all of it. Keep that file somewhere safe. If you ever need it back, use Import and choose that file (this replaces everything currently on the page, so it'll ask you to type "yes" to make sure).

16. That's Everything!

You can always come back to this guide by tapping the ? at the top of the page. Now go make this page yours.
